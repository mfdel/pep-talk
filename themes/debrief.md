# Theme: `debrief` — bank the rep

Shared rules: `themes/guide.md` (read first). Length ~6 min. Default mode: sleep. Wake allowed the
morning after a late event.

## Intent

For the evening after any visible act: a talk, an interview, a post, a hard conversation, a first
race, a message to someone you admire. The cost people fear most after a visible act is often not
the act; it is the replay afterwards, and the habit of filing wins as luck so they never protect
the next act. This speech names the act in one plain sentence, sets the prediction beside what
actually happened, savours one moment that went right, banks the act on the action scoreboard with
one honest test ("If it had gone badly, would you have counted it against yourself? Then it counts
for you now."), lets the listener say "I did that." themselves, and lets the day go.

It does **not replay the content** of what they said; that is the rumination loop. And it ends with
a prompt to write the 4-line log themselves (Feared / Actual / Learned / I did that.), never as a
substitute for it.

- **Best for:** the evening after a talk, an interview, a call, a post, a hard conversation or a
  message to a person; a raw day that followed a visible act.
- **Not for:** days without a visible act (use `gratitude-winddown`); a rejection that arrived today
  (use `compassion`); mornings before an event (use `rehearsal`).

## Evidence basis

| Element | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Distanced "Alex, you…" voice after an evaluated act | Linked to less post-event processing and shame after a speech task | Kross et al. 2014 |
| Prediction vs what happened, no replay of content | Post-event processing and self-focused review maintain social fear | Clark & Wells 1995 |
| Name the skill as practised, bank the act | Effort-based framing protects motivation after setbacks; mastery experiences build self-efficacy | Mueller & Dweck 1998; Bandura 1997 |
| One rule both ways for wins and losses | If a bad outcome would have counted against you, a good one counts for you; it repairs the habit of filing wins as luck | design choice, not a study |

## Structure

Pace ≈ 130 wpm. **430–480 spoken words** (≈ 3.5 min) + **≈ 150 s of `[[PAUSE n]]`** ≈ 6 min.
Characters 2,700–3,300.

| # | Section | Words | Pause budget | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive | 45–70 | ~20 s | Name, breaths with one `[sighing]`. "The act is done. Tonight we bank it." |
| 2 | The act, plainly | 40–60 | ~15 s | One plain sentence: what they did, where, when. No content of what they said |
| 3 | Prediction vs what happened | 80–100 | ~25 s | What they predicted beforehand, then what actually happened, from the prompt or the brief only. No verdict on the gap; let it stand |
| 4 | Savour one moment | 70–90 | ~25 s | One moment that went right. One sense. Stay there; savouring, not replaying |
| 5 | Bank it | 70–90 | ~20 s | The act on the action scoreboard whatever the reply. The both-ways test. Name the skill as practised. Then the silence where the listener says "I did that." themselves, `[[PAUSE 6]]` |
| 6 | The log, then let go | 80–100 | ~35 s | If the four lines are not written yet: two minutes in phone notes, in their own words. Then softer, slower: the day is done. `[whispering]`, then `[sleepy drowsy voice]`. End on `[[PAUSE 30]]` |

## Endings

- `sleep` — no count-up, no plan beyond the log. Last chunks `[whispering]` → `[sleepy drowsy
  voice]`. End on `[[PAUSE 30]]`.
- `wake` (morning after a late event) — count up 1 → 3, `[softly]` → plain → plain, no `[excited]`.
  The one action is the 4-line log if it is not written. Last pause ≤ 5 s.

## Brief must contain

- §1 routing (a raw evening may route to `compassion`).
- §3: the act in one plain sentence, with where and when, and the skill to name as practised.
- §5: the prediction beforehand and what actually happened (from the prompt or the notes), plus
  one moment to savour with its sense; if the act was work, any reply so far, with whom by role.
- §6: the critic's line, only if it is already filing the win as luck (max one).
- §9 their own lines; §14 never mention; §15 do not say.
- **Fallback** if the brief is `INSUFFICIENT`: invite mode — "let the moment you began come back",
  "what did you expect this morning?", each followed by 10–12 s of silence.

The brief agent finds this best in today's journal entry or notes, the prompt itself, and any
earlier note where the listener wrote down what they feared about this event.

## Language specific to this theme

- The act is past tense; the savoured moment may be present tense ("you are standing…").
- No content replay: never quote or rate what they said in the talk, the post or the interview.
- No grading ("it went well", "a strong answer"). The act counts because it was done.
- Reply or no reply, the act is banked. Never "and they loved it".

## Affirmations

None new. The ownership line "I did that." and the both-ways test are fixed parts of this theme,
spoken in section 5. Nothing new goes to `speeches/affirmations.md`. Skip the log, unless section 5
used one of the listener's own lines from the brief for the first time; then log that line as
`own words`.

## Worked example — sections 3 to 5 (excerpt), `mode: sleep`, after a hard conversation with a manager

```
# 3. Prediction and what happened
[softly] This morning, part of you predicted the one-to-one would go badly, Alex.
A cold face. A long silence.
[[PAUSE 3]]
[slowly] What happened was simpler. You said the workload is too much.
Your manager listened, and you agreed to go through the list together on Thursday.
Let both sit side by side… the prediction, and the afternoon as it was.
[[PAUSE 6]]
# 4. One moment
[softly] Find one moment that went right. Maybe the second before you spoke, when you decided to say it anyway.
Notice your hands on the table. Stay there for a breath.
[[PAUSE 8]]
# 5. Bank it
[slowly] If it had gone badly, would you have counted it against yourself?
Then it counts for you now, Alex. Saying the hard thing is a skill, and you are training it.
[[PAUSE 4]]
[softly] Say it now, in your own voice. Out loud, or silently. I did that.
[[PAUSE 6]]
```

## Don'ts

- No replay or review of the content of what they said; no "next time you could…".
- No reach, applause, likes or replies as the measure; the act is the measure.
- No critic work beyond one noticed line; if the evening is raw, the brief routes to `compassion`.
- No substitute for the written log; the speech prompts it.
- No wake-only tags in sleep mode; no scene cues except one `[bell ringing]` at the very start.
