# Theme: `evidence-reel` — the person who did that is here now

Shared rules: `themes/guide.md` (read first). Length ~10 min. Default mode: wake. Sleep allowed.

## Intent

A harsh inner critic rarely denies the record; it disowns it. Wins get filed as luck, as the tool,
as "anyone could have done it", as someone else's. This theme replays three real episodes from the
brief, slowly and with the senses, names the skill used in each **as practised**, and closes the gap
between "the person who did that" and "the person breathing here now". It ends by collecting the
skills into one sentence and stating the affirmations as evidence, not hope.

The episodes are **episodes of choice, effort or fear acted through**, never talent markers (no exam
rank, no "you're smart"). Talent praise feeds the belief that needing effort means you are not good
enough; effort and choice do not.

- **Best for:** self-diminishing or disowning feelings; imposter feelings; before sending an
  application, a pitch, a post or a hard message; before a technical round; a season of interviews
  with no date yet.
- **Not for:** a fresh rejection or a raw day (use `compassion` first); comparison with others (use
  `values-anchor`); a dated event within 7 days (use `rehearsal`); any topic for which three real
  episodes are not in the brief.

## Variant: week reel

For a quiet week: things went out (applications, pitches, messages, a post) and nothing came back.
Same structure and budget. The three episodes are **this week's three acts within the listener's
control** (a message sent, a piece of work shipped, a conversation held), each banked on the action
scoreboard whatever the reply. The collect section closes on a fact from the brief about what has
worked before, if the notes hold one ("the last two replies came from messages to a person, not
from forms"), so the tactic stands even when the week is quiet. A composed line that fits here: "A
quiet week tells you about the inbox. It does not tell you about you."

## Evidence basis

| Element | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Relive real past episodes in detail | Mastery experiences are the strongest source of self-efficacy | Bandura 1977, 1997 |
| Name the skill as practised, not as a trait | Praise for ability undermined motivation after setbacks; praise for effort did not | Mueller & Dweck 1998 |
| Affirm with evidence, not global praise | Global positive statements backfire for low self-esteem; specific evidence does not | Wood, Perunovic & Lee 2009 |
| "The person who did that is here now" | Directly counters disowning: the record is theirs | design choice built on the rows above |

## Structure

Pace ≈ 130 wpm. **760–840 spoken words** (≈ 6 min) + **≈ 240 s of `[[PAUSE n]]`** ≈ 10 min.
Characters 4,600–5,500.

| # | Section | Words | Pause budget | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrival | 45–70 | ~20 s | Name, three breaths, body settles. "Tonight / this morning we look at the record." |
| 2 | Episode 1 | 140–160 | ~40 s | Where and when. Invite the senses ("let the room come back"). The moment of choice and the act. Name the skill as practised. "The person who did that is the person breathing here now." |
| 3 | Episode 2 | 140–160 | ~40 s | Same shape, a different domain or period of life |
| 4 | Episode 3 | 140–160 | ~40 s | The most recent one (last ~60 days), to connect the record to now |
| 5 | Collect | 80–100 | ~35 s | The three skills in one sentence, at fair value: "Luck can pick the timing. You did the work." / "The tool typed it. You decided what it should do, and you checked it." Then the silence where the listener says "I did that." themselves |
| 6 | Affirmations as evidence | 90–110 | ~30 s | The "objection allowed" line, then two or three lines, each 3 times |
| 7 | Return | 60–80 | ~20–30 s | Mode-specific, below |

**Choosing the episodes:** three, from different domains or periods. At least one where fear was
present and they acted anyway; at least one where a practised skill carried them; one recent. Each
must be in the brief with enough fact to retell: what, where, what they did. At most one number per
episode, in spoken form, with the source's verb; give the rest of the words to the moment of choice.
If fewer than three qualify, stop and ask (unattended: switch to `compassion` or `future-self` and
say why in the top comment).

**Episode rules:**

- **A widely seen post, talk or project:** the episode is the act (wrote it, stood up, shipped it),
  never the reach, the followers, the likes or who shared it.
- **A leadership or team episode** (a hard reorganisation, a difficult hire, a project rescued):
  frame it as the act, a hard process run carefully. No outcome claim beyond what the sources say.
- **No timeline flourishes** the sources do not hold ("in just four months"). The act carries the
  weight.
- Every fact passes the brief's do-not-say list (§15).

## Endings

- `wake` — count up 1 → 3, `[softly]` → plain → `[excited]`. One action for today that uses one of
  the named skills, as "When X, you do Y" (brief §12, dated). Last pause ≤ 5 s.
- `sleep` — no count-up. The collect sentence repeated once `[slowly]`, once `[whispering]`, then
  `[sleepy drowsy voice]` on the last chunk. End on `[[PAUSE 30]]`.

## Brief must contain

- §3: two or three episodes (week reel: this week's three acts, dated), each with date, place,
  moment of choice, the listener's act at fair value, what followed (verified only), details on
  record or "invite memory", the skill to name, fear present y/n. If fewer than three work or study
  episodes fit, episodes from another domain (a race finished, a hard conversation held, a skill
  learned from scratch).
- For the week reel: any fact about what has worked before.
- §9 their own lines; §11 vetted affirmations and the fair-value collect line; §12 a dated next
  action; §15 do-not-say list.
- **Fallback** if the brief is `INSUFFICIENT`: see "Choosing the episodes".

The brief agent finds this best in a wins or brag log, a CV, project notes, performance reviews,
journal entries about hard days handled, and past speeches (to avoid reusing the same episodes).

## Affirmations

Two or three, each stated as evidence, past tense first, then present: "You have already… You
can…". Each 3 times, plain → `[slowly]` → with the name. No loop of five. Their own lines first.

- "You have already turned a failing launch into three clear steps. You can do it again."
- "You have already spoken up before you felt ready. Ready was never the requirement."
- "Luck can pick the timing. You did the work."

## Worked example — section 2 (excerpt), `mode: wake`

```
# 2. Episode one
[softly] Go back to last March, Alex. Two days before the new booking app goes live.
The data migration fails overnight, and the team chat fills up with red.
[[PAUSE 4]]
[slowly] Let the room come back. The screen, the coffee, whatever you remember of the light.
Notice the moment you see it: nobody has a plan yet.
[[PAUSE 5]]
[softly] And you write one. Three steps on the whiteboard.
Roll back. Fix the script. Rerun one table at a time.
[[PAUSE 3]]
[slowly] The app goes live on the day the team promised.
[[PAUSE 4]]
[softly] That skill has a name, Alex. Turning a panic into steps. You trained it.
[[PAUSE 3]]
[slowly] The person who did that… is the person breathing here now.
[[PAUSE 6]]
```

## Don'ts

- Never invent an episode, a quote, a number or a reaction. Invite memory; do not assert sensory
  details the brief does not hold.
- No "you were lucky", "with help from", "the team did most of it" softeners; credit sits with them
  at fair value, and with the team where the sources say so.
- No over-claims either: not "Not luck. Not the tool. You.", not "a problem nobody else could
  solve". Use the fair-value forms above.
- No talent markers (exam ranks, "gifted", "smart"). No reach or follower counts.
- No comparison ("better than most"). No future scene; this theme stays on the record.
- No other person as the hero of any episode. The evidence belongs to the listener.
