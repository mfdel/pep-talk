# Theme: `values-anchor` — back to what you stand for

Shared rules: `themes/guide.md` (read first). Length ~8 min. Default mode: as the prompt says; else
sleep for an evening request, wake for a morning one.

## Intent

For days when the ground moves: scrolling someone else's life or career, an offer to weigh, a fork
in the road, flatness at work. The speech does not argue with the comparison or pick the option. It
brings two of the listener's own core values into the room, relives one real moment of living each,
and asks what those values say about the choice in front of them. It ends on one anchoring sentence,
repeated.

**An identity wobble** ("I don't even know what I am any more") is not always an open question. If
the profile or the notes record a direction the listener has already chosen, in their own words, the
speech **restates that direction** and does not ask. The open question in section 5 is only for
genuine decisions: an offer, a fork, a move.

- **Best for:** upward comparison, decision anxiety, an offer or a move to weigh, an identity
  wobble, flatness or boredom at work, feeling behind.
- **Not for:** doubt about a specific skill or task (use `evidence-reel`); a fresh rejection (use
  `compassion`); a dated event (use `rehearsal`).

Weakest evidence base of the set: the right tool for comparison days, not a default.

## Evidence basis

| Element | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Reflect on core values when the self feels threatened | Self-affirmation restores a sense of integrity and lowers defensive responses | Steele 1988 |
| Affirm a value **unrelated to the threatened domain** | The classic design; effects in large replications are smaller and context-dependent | Cohen & Sherman 2014 |
| Values, not traits ("you value honesty", not "you are brilliant") | Global praise backfires for low self-esteem | Wood, Perunovic & Lee 2009 |

## Structure

Pace ≈ 130 wpm. **610–670 spoken words** (≈ 5 min) + **≈ 190 s of `[[PAUSE n]]`** ≈ 8 min.
Characters 3,800–4,600.

| # | Section | Words | Pause budget | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive | 45–70 | ~20 s | Name, breaths, one `[sighing]` release. Body settles |
| 2 | The wobble, named | 60–80 | ~20 s | Plainly: the comparison or the decision. Never name or describe the other person; "someone else's path" is enough |
| 3 | Value one | 130–150 | ~40 s | Name the value in their own words. One real moment of living it, from the brief; invite memory. What it cost, what they chose |
| 4 | Value two | 130–150 | ~40 s | Same shape. **Must** come from a domain unrelated to the wobble (a work wobble → a value lived outside work) |
| 5 | What the values say | 90–110 | ~25 s | Decision: values set the criterion, not the answer. One question ("Which path lets you stay honest and keep building?"), then `[[PAUSE 15]]`–`[[PAUSE 20]]`. Identity wobble: no question; restate the chosen direction in their words, then the silence where the listener says it themselves |
| 6 | Anchor line | 60–80 | ~15 s | One sentence, three times: plain → `[slowly]` → with the name or `[whispering]` (sleep) |
| 7 | Return | 50–70 | ~15–25 s | Mode-specific, below |

## Endings

- `sleep` — no decision tonight. "The values will still be here in the morning." Last chunk
  `[sleepy drowsy voice]`, end on `[[PAUSE 30]]`.
- `wake` — count up 1 → 3, `[softly]` → plain → `[excited]`. One values-consistent action for today
  as "When X, you do Y". Last pause ≤ 5 s.

## Brief must contain

- §1 routing; whether the wobble is a decision or an identity wobble (identity → restate the
  chosen direction, from the profile or notes, verbatim).
- §10: two values phrased the way the listener phrases them, one of them unrelated to the threatened
  domain, each with one dated moment of living it (with §3 detail for that moment).
- For decisions: any decisions already made, as criteria, so the speech does not reopen them.
- §9 their own lines; §11 vetted anchor candidates; §12 a dated next action (wake); §14 never
  mention; §15 do not say (comparison, titles, salaries).
- **Fallback** if the brief is `INSUFFICIENT`: `compassion`, and say why in the top comment.

Value families the brief agent can recognise in the listener's notes (use their words, not these
labels):

- **Honesty / accuracy / fair value** — saying what is true, owning the real size of things.
- **Building / craft** — making things that work and last.
- **Courage and agency** — acting before feeling ready.
- **Care and connection** — showing up for people.
- **Growth and learning** — getting better at something on purpose.
- **Family** — only as a quiet, unconditional value, never a goal, a reward or a performance arena.

The brief agent finds this best in the profile's values, journal entries about choices made, and
notes where the listener explains why something mattered to them.

## Affirmations

No loop. One anchoring sentence that joins the two values, e.g.:

- "You choose what is honest, and you keep building."
- "Your path is measured against your values, not against anyone's timeline."
- Identity wobble: the listener's own sentence for the direction they chose, verbatim.

Log it in `speeches/affirmations.md` (guide §7).

## Worked example — section 3 (excerpt), `mode: sleep`, comparison with a friend's promotion

```
# 3. Value one
[softly] The first thing you stand for, Alex… is honesty.
Saying what is true, even when the room would rather not hear it.
[[PAUSE 4]]
[slowly] Go back to June. The Monday project meeting. Every update so far has been on track.
Then it is your turn, and your part is two weeks behind.
[[PAUSE 4]]
[softly] Let the room come back, whatever you remember of it.
You say it plainly. Two weeks behind, and here is why.
[[PAUSE 5]]
[slowly] A short silence. Then the whole table works out a new plan.
That is what honesty looks like when it costs something.
[[PAUSE 4]]
[softly] Someone else's path cannot tell you whether you were honest today.
Only you can. That measure is yours.
[[PAUSE 6]]
```

## Don'ts

- No comparison in either direction; never "you are ahead of them", never their title or salary.
- No making the decision for them; the values frame the choice, the listener makes it.
- No reopening a direction the listener has already chosen; on an identity wobble, restate it.
- No more than two values; depth beats a list.
- Values are things they care about, not traits they have ("you value courage", not "you are
  brave").
