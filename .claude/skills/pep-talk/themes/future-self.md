# Theme: `future-self` — the quantum jump

Shared rules: `themes/guide.md` (read first). Length ~10 min. Default mode: sleep for personal
goals, wake for work goals.

## Intent

The imaginative frame is **quantum jumping** (Burt Goldman): relax, cross a threshold into a reality
a few months from now, observe the listener living there, ask one question, bring one quality back.
Keep the frame whole and sincere. The vivid scene comes first; then the real inner obstacle and an
if-then plan turn the fantasy into effort instead of a substitute for it. **The obstacle and the
if-then can never be dropped**, in any mode or variant.

The future self is **near and ordinary**: weeks to months away, on a normal Tuesday, working a goal
the listener controls. Not "the Alex who already made it", not an apex, not a triumph. For goals
that depend on someone else's decision (a job offer, a promotion, an acceptance letter), the goal in
the scene is a **controllable practice goal** ("the Alex who describes their work at fair value",
"the Alex whose three runs a week are routine"); the outcome appears at most as a one-line glimpse,
and the scene shows the **role, never a named live company**.

- **Best for:** a goal weeks to months away (a role, a habit, a skill, a race, a language); days
  that need direction.
- **Not for:** a raw day (use `compassion`); low expectancy of success (use `compassion` or
  `debrief`); a dated event in the next 7 days (use `rehearsal`).

**Expectancy check.** Before writing, read the brief §1. If expectancy is low or the day is raw, do
not write this theme, even when the topic fits; route per the brief. Mental contrasting energises at
reasonable expectancy and disengages at low expectancy.

## Evidence basis

| Element | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Scene → obstacle → if-then (WOOP order) | Contrasting the wish with the obstacle energises; fantasy alone drains effort. Job-seeking graduates who fantasised more got fewer offers and lower salaries | Oettingen & Mayer 2002; Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 |
| Only at reasonable expectancy | Mental contrasting at low expectancy tends to produce disengagement | Oettingen, WOOP |
| Show what the future self did to get there | Process simulation beats outcome simulation | Pham & Taylor 1999 |
| A near, ordinary, similar future self | Connection to a vivid, similar future self supports present action | Hershfield et al. 2011 |
| The quality brought back is already theirs | Counters disowning; builds on real mastery | Bandura 1997 |

## Structure

Pace ≈ 130 wpm. **750–850 spoken words** (≈ 6 min) + **≈ 220 s of `[[PAUSE n]]`** ≈ 9–10 min.
Characters 4,500–5,500.

| # | Section | Words | Pause budget | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrival | 45–70 | ~20 s | Greet them by name. Two slow breaths (`[sighing]` once as release). One body cue: jaw, shoulders, hands in a single line. Countdown 3 → 1 |
| 2 | Softening the critic | 90–110 | ~25 s | Name the harsh voice gently and specifically (one pattern from the brief, in plain words). Thank it for trying to protect them. Ask it to sit in the back for ten minutes |
| 3 | The jump | 250–290 | ~75 s | Threshold (a door, a corridor, a garden gate, a station platform). Step through, a few months ahead. An ordinary day in the goal from brief §2. Meet the future self, named as such ("the future Alex"). Say what the goal is in one plain sentence. Sensory detail: light, sound, temperature, posture, one object. Process details from the action scoreboard, each with when and what ("in October, twenty minutes every morning before work"). Any outcome glimpse says plainly what it is ("the job offer for this role"). You, the listener of today, ask the future self **one** question, then `[[PAUSE 20]]`–`[[PAUSE 30]]`. Receive one quality |
| 4 | Obstacle + if-then | 110–130 | ~25 s | **Never dropped.** The future self names the inner obstacle from the brief, calmly. Then one or two "When X, you do Y" plans from the brief, in their voice |
| 5 | Affirmation loop | 130–160 | ~45 s | The "objection allowed" line, then three affirmations (the evidence one preceded once by its full anchor: where, when, what), each spoken 3 times. The silence where the listener says one line themselves goes here |
| 6 | Return | 80–100 | ~25–35 s | Mode-specific, below |

## Endings

- `sleep` — no counting up, no energy rise. Slower, softer, shorter sentences, longer pauses. The
  quality settles into the body. Last chunks `[softly]` → `[whispering]` → `[sleepy drowsy voice]`.
  End on `[[PAUSE 30]]`. Keep section 4 short and resolved; never end near the obstacle.
- `wake` — count up 1 → 5: `[softly]` on 1–2, plain on 3, `[excited]` on 4–5. Eyes open on 5. End
  with **one concrete action for today**, in if-then form, from brief §12 (dated, checked against
  today). Last pause ≤ 5 s. One `[applause]` scene cue is allowed at the success moment in section 3
  in wake mode only.

## Brief must contain

- §1 expectancy reasonable (else suggest another theme).
- §2: the goal in one plain sentence (a role, not a named company); two or three activities of an
  ordinary day once it is reached; the action scoreboard so far (up to three dated acts within the
  listener's control); the inner obstacle in their own words; outer obstacles.
- §3: one origin episode, for the evidence affirmation.
- §11: three vetted affirmation candidates (process, evidence, value), their own lines first.
- §12: one or two if-then plans, and a dated next action for wake mode.
- §13: imagery to use and avoid (no apex imagery). §14 never mention. §15 do not say.
- **Fallback** if the brief is `INSUFFICIENT`: use `compassion` (raw day) or `evidence-reel`, and
  say why in the top comment.

The brief agent finds this best in goal and planning notes, a wins log, a journal's recent entries
and any notes where the listener describes what holds them back.

## Language specific to this theme

- **The future self is always the listener**, a few months on. Never an admired figure, never a
  mentor, never an alter ego. Refer to them as "the future Alex", "this Alex" or "the Alex a few
  months from now", never a bare "they" or "them": the listener must always know who is meant. Never
  a new name, never "the Alex who already made it".
- Present tense in the scene: "The future Alex closes the laptop…". Never "you will" outside the
  if-then plan.
- The payoff pairs any outcome with the action scoreboard: acts within their control that reach a
  person or ship a thing. Never passive volume such as applications sent.
- The question "what did you stop doing, to get here?" works well: subtractive, about process.

## Affirmations

Exactly three, from the brief's vetted candidates, their own lines first:

1. **Process / in-progress** — "You are becoming someone who…", "You are learning to…"
2. **Evidence-backed** — anchored in a real past act from the brief, the skill named as practised.
3. **Value-based** — tied to a value they already hold (brief §10).

Delivery: the "objection allowed" line first. Each affirmation 3 times. Vary: plain → `[slowly]` →
with the name or `[whispering]`. One repetition may be the interrogative form. 3–4 s `[[PAUSE n]]`
after each for silent repetition.

## Worked example — section 3 (excerpt), `mode: sleep`, learning a language

```
# 3. The jump
[softly] Ahead of you there is a door… plain wood, warm under your hand.
On the other side, a few months from now. An ordinary Saturday morning.
[[PAUSE 4]]
[slowly] You step through. The air is warmer here… and full of voices.
A market street. Crates of oranges. Someone calling out prices in Spanish.
[[PAUSE 4]]
[softly] And standing at the bread stall… is the future Alex. You, a few months from now.
Shoulders down. Nothing to prove.
[[PAUSE 5]]
[slowly] The future Alex asks for two loaves, in Spanish, and asks about the olives too.
The answer comes back fast. The future Alex follows most of it, and asks again for the rest.
[[PAUSE 5]]
[slowly] The future Alex got here one lesson at a time. In October, fifteen minutes every morning,
before coffee. Two calls a week with a tutor, even in the weeks that felt slow.
[[PAUSE 5]]
[softly] Now you, the Alex of today, ask the future Alex one question.
Alex… what did you stop doing, to get here?
[[PAUSE 25]]
[slowly] Whatever came… let it be enough. Even silence is an answer.
[[PAUSE 4]]
[softly] Now the future Alex hands you one thing to carry back: patience with your own mistakes.
Take it. [pause] It was always yours.
[[PAUSE 8]]
```

## Don'ts

- No meeting a hero, a celebrity, a boss who "finally sees you", or an alter ego.
- No scene where strangers confirm specialness without the actions that led there.
- No named live company or real person in the scene.
- No obstacle left open at the end; no obstacle at all in the final three minutes of a sleep speech.
  The obstacle is never removed to fix that; it moves earlier and stays short.
- No more than one question to the future self; the silence after it is the point.
