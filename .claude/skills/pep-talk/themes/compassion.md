# Theme: `compassion` — the critic and the compassionate self

Shared rules: `themes/guide.md` (read first). Length ~8 min. Default mode: sleep. Wake allowed.

## Intent

For the evening after something stung: a rejection, a mistake, a cringe moment, a harsh day. The
goal is not to fix, reframe or plan. It is to name what happened plainly, notice the critic and
where it sits in the body, remember that this is what trying feels like for anyone, and then let the
listener's own compassionate self speak: wise, strong and warm at once. It ends with permission and
one small kind act, and a single anchor line instead of an affirmation loop.

Register: plain, warm, factual, brief. No sweetness. Highly self-critical people often find
compassion uncomfortable at first; that discomfort is normal and the speech can say so once.

- **Best for:** a harsh day; right after a rejection, a ghosting, a mistake at work, a public
  stumble; friction with a colleague; nights when the critic is loud. The default theme for a raw
  day.
- **Not for:** goal pumping or motivation (use `future-self`); planning an event (use `rehearsal`);
  days that are merely flat (use `gratitude-winddown`).

## Evidence basis

| Element | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Kindness, common humanity, mindful noticing | The three components of self-compassion; linked to resilience without lowering standards | Neff 2003 |
| The compassionate self: wise, strong, warm | Builds a soothing system to balance the threat system that drives harsh self-criticism | Gilbert 2009 |
| Compassion after failure, not pep | Self-compassion after a failure increased motivation to improve and study time | Breines & Chen 2012 |

## Structure

Pace ≈ 130 wpm. **600–660 spoken words** (≈ 4.8 min) + **≈ 190 s of `[[PAUSE n]]`** ≈ 8 min.
Characters 3,700–4,500.

| # | Section | Words | Pause budget | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive | 45–70 | ~20 s | Name, breaths with one `[sighing]` release. Permission to stop: nothing to solve tonight |
| 2 | Name what happened | 70–90 | ~20 s | Plain facts from the prompt or the brief. No spin, no silver lining, no lesson |
| 3 | The critic | 90–110 | ~30 s | At most two of the critic's words from the brief, framed as noticing a thought rather than obeying it ("the thought 'you always mess this up' showed up"), never the last sentence before a pause. Where they land in the body: jaw, chest, stomach. "That is the old alarm." Do not argue with it. One `[sharp exhale]` allowed to let the words go |
| 4 | Common humanity | 70–90 | ~20 s | Anyone who applies, speaks, ships, asks, gets this. Not alone. No ranking ("others have it worse") |
| 5 | The compassionate self | 140–160 | ~45 s | The listener at their wisest, strongest, warmest. Their posture, their voice. They speak three or four sentences to the listener. One plain fact from the record only if the brief marks it allowed |
| 6 | Hand on chest | 50–70 | ~20 s | Invite a hand on the chest or cheek, or just noticing the breath. Warmth, weight, breath |
| 7 | Permission + small kind act + anchor | 70–90 | ~20–30 s | Permission to feel it. One small kind act (sleep now; a glass of water; a walk tomorrow). The anchor line, three times; the last time is the silence where the listener says it themselves |

## Endings

- `sleep` — the anchor line `[softly]` → `[slowly]` → `[whispering]`; last chunk `[sleepy drowsy
  voice]`; end on `[[PAUSE 30]]`.
- `wake` — no count-up energy rise. The small kind act becomes one gentle step for today, as "When
  X, you do Y". Last pause ≤ 5 s. No `[excited]`, `[playful]` or `[chuckling]` even in wake mode.

## Brief must contain

- §1 routing and day state.
- §5: plain facts of what happened (what, when, stage, any stated reason verbatim), and whether one
  plain fact may appear in the wise voice.
- §6: at most two critic lines, verbatim if the listener wrote them down, with source.
- §7 reframe; §8 the compassionate-voice register, two or three sentences it could say, and words
  to avoid.
- §9 their own lines; §11 anchor candidates, their own lines first.
- §14 never mention (the compassionate self is rest energy, not performance energy); §15 do not say.
- **Fallback** if the brief is `INSUFFICIENT`: invite mode — "let what happened come back, just the
  facts", with no facts asserted.

**A plain fact in the wise voice.** One plain fact from the record (a rejection's stated reason,
the stage reached, the fact that the decision was made before the interview) corrects a misreport;
it is not a silver lining. It may appear in section 5 only, once. Never in section 2.

**Self-blame after a rejection or an interview:** when the brief places it, the anchor can be the
listener's own learning line, in the shape "I didn't know then what this taught me. Now I do." It
is for the self-blame stage only; the sadness itself gets no line.

The brief agent finds this best in recent journal entries, the profile's critic patterns and the
lines that help, and any email or note about what happened.

## Anchor line

One line, no loop. It names a fact and offers kindness, and survives the critic. Examples:

- "One rejection is one data point. You are still the person who did the work."
- "You tried in public. That was the rep, and the rep counts."
- "This hurts because it mattered. It is not a verdict."
- "Hard day. Kind voice. Both are true."

Log it in `.pep-talk/affirmations.md` (guide §7).

## Worked example — section 5 (excerpt), `mode: sleep`, after a rejection email

```
# 5. The compassionate self
[softly] Now let another part of you come forward, Alex.
The part of you that is wise… strong… and warm, all at once.
[[PAUSE 5]]
[slowly] Notice how this part of you sits beside you. Unhurried.
It has seen hard days before, and it is not afraid of this one.
[[PAUSE 5]]
[softly] Listen to what it says.
That hurt. Of course it hurt. You wanted that job, and you went for it.
[[PAUSE 4]]
[slowly] One email is not a verdict on you. It is one reply, from one room. Not from every room.
[[PAUSE 4]]
[softly] You do not have to earn rest tonight. You already did the work.
[[PAUSE 6]]
[whispering] Let that voice be yours. It always was.
[[PAUSE 8]]
```

## Don'ts

- No affirmation loop, no goal imagery, no if-then planning beyond one small kind act.
- No premature reframe ("this is a blessing", "everything happens for a reason", "what's the
  lesson?").
- No "it's not your fault" when the brief does not support it. Kindness, not a verdict either way.
- No outside figure as the comforter: the compassionate self is the listener. No family scenes
  scripted for effect; family only as unconditional warmth.
- No wake-only tags, no scene cues except optionally one `[heartbeat]` under the hand-on-chest
  section.
